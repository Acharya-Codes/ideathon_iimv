The opportunity

One supply challenge.
Two sides that need value.

India needs resilient access to critical minerals. Producer countries want investment, skills, local value addition and responsible development—not only raw-material exports.

◈

For Indian buyers

Coordinate compatible demand, assess supplier risk and build a clearer route to reliable long-term supply.

↔

For producer partners

Offer a credible base demand commitment and a transparent path to additional volumes when agreed conditions are met.

♻

For the value chain

Link sourcing with local capability-building, traceability and a realistic future option for recovered materials.

How the contract works

Bankable base supply.
Two keys to step up.

The base contract follows normal commercial discipline. Only the additional supply tranche depends on the agreed verification gates.

The two keys
01 Commercial readiness

Evidence of capacity, product quality, delivery performance and technical requirements defined in the contract.

02 Verified shared value

Progress against a small number of agreed local-capability and environmental safeguards, with independent verification.

Both gates must pass. If a gate fails, the parties follow pre-agreed cure periods, remedies or deferment rules. No vague ESG score alone triggers expansion.
Contract building blocks

What must be written into the deal?

Specific terms make the mechanism usable—not just aspirational.

Base offtakeVolume, grade, quality, delivery, pricing formula and remedies.
Expansion trancheVolume cap, option/commitment design, price formula and activation date.
Verification protocolEvidence, verifier independence, audit rights, thresholds and cure period.
Local-value mechanismDefined skills / supplier / processing milestone and agreed funding, if any.
Risk allocationPrice movement, delay, force majeure, termination and dispute resolution.
Circular-material optionSeparate qualified material, availability, quality and commercial conditions.

Design principle: keep the base supply sufficiently bankable; do not make every delivery conditional on every sustainability metric.

Commercial model

An asset-light B2B service.

ORE2LOOP would coordinate qualification, verification and deal workflows; it would not own mines or promise to guarantee supply.

Risks and safeguards

What could go wrong—and how we respond.

A credible proposal explains its limits and designs for them.

RiskWhy it mattersProposed mitigation
Not sufficiently novelOfftake options, ESG-linked finance and demand aggregation already exist in related forms.Benchmark existing contracts; claim novelty only for a validated combination and precise mechanism.
Buyer coordination / competition lawCompetitors' volumes, prices and sourcing plans can be sensitive.Use clean-team data controls, limited aggregation, independent advice and competition-law review.
Milestone disputesVague social or environmental KPIs are difficult to verify consistently.Define evidence, baseline, verifier, audit rights, cure period and dispute resolution in advance.
Producer financingUncertain expansion volumes may not support project finance.Preserve bankable base offtake; negotiate option fees or capacity reservations if needed.
Price / project volatilityCommodity cycles, permits, production and shipping can change economics.Use transparent pricing formulas, volume limits, force-majeure provisions and scenario analysis.
Unrealistic circularityRecycled lithium supply depends on available feedstock, recovery and qualification.Make secondary material a separate optional pathway; count actual procured material only.
Chile context and consentLocal law, state participation, water and community concerns can affect project viability.Screen project-specific rules, consult affected stakeholders and require applicable approvals.
